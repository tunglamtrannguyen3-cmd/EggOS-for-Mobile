## 🪟 Microsoft

Windows is not an operating system.  
It is a **store that happens to boot**.

I turn on my PC and the first thing it does is advertise to me.  
On hardware I already paid for.

That is not a feature.  
That is a street performer who followed me home.

- The Start menu is a mall
- Search is a salesman
- Settings is two apps wearing the same nametag, giving different directions, then blaming me when I get lost

I asked for **Calculator**.

Windows heard:

```text
please recommend Copilot
please recommend OneDrive
please recommend Edge
please recommend a Microsoft account
please restart in 12 minutes
```

Telemetry knows what I clicked, when I clicked it, and probably how I feel about the weather widget.

Privacy settings exist so you can feel like you did something while the vacuum is still on.

Old features do not get removed.  
They get **"reimagined."**

Which means:

1. worse
2. slower
3. now it wants an account

> You did not ship an OS.  
> You shipped a loyalty program with a taskbar.

**EggOS will have windows.**  
It will not have a pitch deck inside them.

## 🤖 Android / Google

Android started as:

> “a phone you can actually own.”

Then Google looked at that sentence like it was a bug.

Freedom got patched.  
Sideloading got a warning label, then another warning label, then a form, then a pep talk about safety.

**Security matters.**

Using security as the excuse to decide what software I am allowed to run on a phone I bought is not security.

That is landlord energy.

| What I wanted | What I got |
| :--- | :--- |
| A camera | Twelve Google apps |
| A browser | Three apps that think they are Messages |
| A result | Shopping, summaries, and a paragraph that did not answer the question |
| A store | Airport security for apps |
| My own app | The suspicious suitcase |

The welcome screen lasts four seconds.  
The account lasts forever.

> If I own the phone, I am the admin.  
> That should not be a radical opinion.  
> That should be the default.

Keep the platform.  
I am building the option that remembers who paid for the device.

## 🍎 Apple

Apple products are pretty.  
Apple prices are a **jump scare**.

The aluminum is perfect.  
The software is smooth.  
The ecosystem is so polished you do not notice the lock until you try to leave.

> **Slogan:** It just works.  
> **Fine print:** It just works if you already own the rest of the family.

**Walled garden?**  
Cute name.

It is a penthouse with no stairs and a $29 cable that only talks to other $29 cables.

- Repairability is treated like a rumor
- The battery is “part of the design”
- The design is part of the brand
- The brand is part of why a cracked screen costs more than a used laptop

I do not hate the hardware.  
I hate that buying it feels like signing a roommate agreement with a corporation.

> Nice garden.  
> EggOS is a backyard.  
> The gate opens.  
> That is the point.

## 🏹 Arch Linux

Arch, I like you.  
That is why this one hurts.

Pacman is clean.  
The wiki is actually good.  
The install makes you feel like you built a computer with your bare hands and a text file.

Then the comments section arrives.

```text
Beginner:  How do I connect to Wi-Fi?
Reply 1:   Read the wiki.
Reply 2:   Skill issue.
Reply 3:   [kernel lecture]
Reply 4:   I use Arch, by the way.
```

That last one is not information.  
That is a mating call.

Not every Arch user is like this.  
The helpful ones are the only reason the stereotype has survivors.

The unhelpful ones treat Linux like a secret club and the wiki like scripture you should already have memorized.

Arch is an operating system.

- It is not a belt rank
- It is not a personality
- It is not a reason to be rude to someone who just wants the internet

**EggOS docs will explain the thing.**  
Then the words in the thing.  
Then an example that does not start with “simply.”

## 🐧 Linux / Linus

Linus, the kernel is the reason half the planet’s computers even remember how to computer.

It is also about to get egg on it.  
Sorry.

Linux is powerful, free, customizable, and present in everything from supercomputers to devices that should not have been given a kernel and a sense of destiny.

The desktop is great until two apps start arguing about:

- file pickers
- dark mode
- whether a system tray is civilization

There are **400** ways to install a package.  
There are **401** blog posts explaining why the other 399 are wrong.  
All of them work on someone else’s machine.

I will follow the license.  
I will keep the notices.  
I will give credit.

I will also name something important after an egg.  
That is the deal.

> If it panics, my fault.  
> If it boots, thank you for the homework.  
> I copied it legally.
